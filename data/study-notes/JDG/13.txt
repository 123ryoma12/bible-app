As happened with the great judge Gideon, the Angel of the Lord appears at his calling, but unlike any other judge, he is called from the womb. He also experiences the Spirit coming upon him more than any other judge (13:25; 14:6, 19; 15:14). Of all the judges, Samson is perhaps the most complex. Having taken the Nazirite vow (13:4, 5, 7, 14; 16:17; cf. Num. 6:1–21), he exhibits through his conduct a growing disregard for God’s covenant and his vow. His interest in delivering Israel seems at times to be overshadowed by his pursuit of pleasure and personal glory. The fanfare of his calling and the circumstances surrounding his birth only heighten the tragedy of his life and emphasize the depths to which Israel has descended. Nevertheless, God raises him up to deliver Israel from the Philistines (v. 5), and He uses even the sins of Samson as an occasion against them (14:4).\n\nThough the Spirit comes upon him repeatedly, this has no apparent impact on his character. Yet the account of the end of his life (16:25–31) reflects a renewal of his faith while a prisoner of his (and God’s) enemies.
