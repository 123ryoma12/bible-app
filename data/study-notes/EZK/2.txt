The phrase emphasizes the prophet’s humanity and frailty, especially as it stands in such proximity to a vision of God’s glory and the heavenly beings that surround Him. It also distinguishes him from his contemporaries, whom the Lord calls “the people (lit. ‘sons’) of Israel.” They are a “nation of rebels,” the true heirs of their ancestor, Jacob/Israel, whose distinguishing feature was struggling with God (Gen. 32:28). As “Son of Adam,” Ezekiel represents the first member of a new community of faith, indwelt by the Spirit and empowered for a life of new obedience (2:2). As happens with the dry bones of ch. 37, when the Spirit comes into the prophet, Ezekiel stands on his feet obediently ready to serve (cf. 37:10). Indwelt by the Spirit, He obeyed and became the last Adam, the founding member of God’s new community of faith (1 Cor. 15).
