Like other letters of the first century, the epistle to the Romans contains an opening (1:1–17), a body (1:18–15:13), and a conclusion (15:14–16:27). Romans, however, unlike most other letters of Paul’s day, is an exceptionally long letter. Its length is not characteristic of most first-century letters, which were much shorter. More importantly, Romans is a distinctive type of epistle. Scholars who have examined ancient Greco-Roman epistolary conventions have distinguished several kinds of letters such as letters of friendship, letters of exhortation, and letters of recommendation.
