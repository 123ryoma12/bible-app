The first eleven chapters of Genesis share many parallels, as well as conscious dissimilarities with ancient Near Eastern myths that preceded the time of Moses (Mesopotamian creation accounts such as the Enuma Elish; flood accounts such as those included in the Atrahasis Epic; and the eleventh tablet of the Gilgamesh Epic). Names and customs in the narratives about the patriarchs (chs. 12–50) accurately reflect that era, suggesting an early author with reliable documents. The Ebla texts (twenty-fourth century B.C.) mention Ebrium, possibly the Eber of Gen. 10:21, and the Mari texts (eighteenth century B.C.) attest to names such as “Abraham,” “Jacob,” and “Amo­rite.” The practice of granting a birthright (i.e., additional privileges to the eldest son, 25:5–6, 32–34; 43:33; 49:3) was widespread in the ancient Near East, and the sale of an inheritance (25:29–34) is documented at different periods in this area. Much of Genesis focuses on the experiences of a semi-nomadic family that lived about 2000 B.C.
