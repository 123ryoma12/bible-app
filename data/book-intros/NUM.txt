Given that it would have been quite difficult to support the needs of such a large group of people for the many years Israel wandered in the desert, and since other ancient kingdoms rarely report armies of that size, some scholars doubt that the census totals reported in the book of Numbers are accurate.","Consequently, a variety of suggestions have been made to address the issue of these apparently inflated numbers. It has been argued that perhaps the figures are not merely a military census, but that they also include women and children. Thus, the number 603,550 is inclusive of the entire nation. Others have suggested that the word for “thousand” actually refers in these chapters to a military unit or group. So, for example, the numbers for the tribe of Reuben (1:21) would actually be “forty-six units (totaling) 500 soldiers.” The entire enrollment of Israel, then, would be 598 units totaling 5,550 soldiers. Others suggest that the census figures derive from a later period in Israel’s history and were then projected back into this earlier episode, but this can be safely rejected given the proven reliability of the biblical account.","None of the aforementioned solutions entirely solves the proposed difficulty.
