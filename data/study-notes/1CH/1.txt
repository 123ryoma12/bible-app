The genealogies sometimes skip generations without notice, emphasizing significant persons and events (6:4–15). Ancient genealogies often included brief narratives highlighting important events, and the writer of Chronicles pauses on occasion to tell a story (4:9,10; 5:18–22).\n\nAncient genealogies set out not only family relations, but political, geographical, and social connections. Chronicles contains an assortment of lists, including families (e.g., 3:17–24), political relations (e.g., 2:24, 42, 45, 49–52), and trade guilds (e.g., 4:14, 21–23).\n\nAn essential purpose of the extensive genealogies in Chronicles is to convince the returned exiles of their true status as the legitimate continuation of God’s elect people.
